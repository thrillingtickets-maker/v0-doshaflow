# DoshaFlow Editorial Benchmark
## "Ayurvedic Cooking Class" — Design & Structure Reference

This document preserves the design, tone, and structure of the Ayurvedic Cooking Class article as the template for all future DoshaFlow editorial content.

---

## EDITORIAL TONE & VOICE

**Core Principles:**
- **Story-first approach**: Begin with personal narrative, anecdote, or lived experience
- **Conversational expertise**: Knowledgeable without being didactic; teach through storytelling
- **Practical application**: Always bridge from theory to actionable takeaways
- **Generational reverence**: Honor traditional knowledge; present it as inherited wisdom, not trends
- **Restrained branding**: Premium product positioning without hard sells; let value speak for itself

**Sentence Structure:**
- Short, impactful statements mixed with longer narrative flows
- Rhetorical questions to engage reader reflection
- Dialogue and direct observation to create immediacy
- Pull quotes that distill key insights into memorable phrases

## METADATA & HEADER STRUCTURE

**Top Section Format:**
```
DOSHAFLOW [CATEGORY]
[SUBCATEGORY] · [ARTICLE TYPE]

[Main Headline — Long Form, Benefit-Driven or Curiosity-Driven]

[2-3 sentence dek that hooks with specificity and promise]

[CATEGORY] [TIME TO READ] [SUBCATEGORY]
```

**Example:**
```
DOSHAFLOW JOURNAL
FIELD NOTES · AYURVEDIC KITCHEN

What I Learned at an Ayurvedic Cooking Class (and Why It Changed How I Think About Food)

Fresh food only. No leftovers. Clay pots. Oils chosen by dish. I spent a day in a South Indian Ayurvedic kitchen and took notes on everything.

DOSHAFLOW JOURNAL · 8 MIN READ · FIELD NOTES
```

## TYPOGRAPHY HIERARCHY & RHYTHM

**Font Treatment:**
- **Headlines:** Serif, bold, premium weight (establishes authority and editorial tone)
- **Body text:** Sans-serif, 16-18px, line-height 1.6-1.8 for breathing room
- **Subheadings:** Serif, font-semibold, slightly larger than body, natural spacing above/below
- **Pull quotes:** Larger serif font, italic or bold, distinctly visually separated
- **Numbered principles:** Large numerals (01, 02) with bold serif headers below, crisp layout
- **Captions:** Small sans-serif, muted color, positioned below/within photos

**Visual Contrast:**
- Clear distinction between narrative sections and principle/recipe boxes
- White space used intentionally to create pause and emphasis
- No more than 2 font families total (serif + sans-serif)

## CONTENT STRUCTURE & PACING

**Standard Flow:**
1. **Hook/Anecdote (300-400 words)**
   - Personal narrative that establishes stakes
   - Sensory detail and emotional authenticity
   - Draws reader into the experience

2. **Key Principle Reveal (100-200 words)**
   - The "stopping point" — the moment that shifts perspective
   - Often a rule or insight that contradicts conventional wisdom
   - Pull quote of main learning

3. **Principles/Breakdown (varied length)**
   - Numbered points (01, 02, 03, etc.)
   - Each principle has bold header + 1-2 sentence explanation
   - Mix of conceptual and practical information

4. **Deep Dive/Application (500-800 words)**
   - Extended story or detailed example
   - Photos placed strategically (not clustered)
   - Natural flow between anecdote and practical detail

5. **Box Content (Recipe/Summary)**
   - Clear visual break from body text
   - Formatted for scannability (bullet points, numbered steps)
   - Practical, immediately useful information
   - Includes source/context when relevant

6. **Integration & Habits (300-500 words)**
   - Connects principles back to reader's life
   - Concrete, small-scale actions (not overwhelming)
   - Acknowledges difficulty/learning curve while staying encouraging

7. **Closing & CTA (50-150 words)**
   - Reflection or forward-looking statement
   - Minimal, restrained product link or related content
   - Never aggressive or high-pressure

## IMAGE PACING & PLACEMENT

**Strategic Image Placement:**
- Hero image implied but not mandatory for all articles
- Images placed after narrative build (don't open with photo)
- Typically 1 image per 400-600 words of content
- Image captions are descriptive but brief
- Photos feel editorial and cinematic, not stock-photography
- Spacing between images prevents visual overload

**Image Types:**
- Behind-the-scenes/documentary style (authenticity over perfection)
- Detail shots (ingredients, hands, textures)
- Finished product shown in context (not isolated)
- Atmosphere/environment to establish place

---

## PULL QUOTE STYLING

**Format & Usage:**
- Extracted directly from narrative text
- Distills insight into single, memorable phrase
- Set in larger serif font (130-150% of body size)
- Often italicized or bolded for emphasis
- Preceded by a thin decorative line (optional)
- Creates visual rhythm break in long-form content
- 1-2 pull quotes per 1500 words of content maximum

**Example:**
```
"Fresh food only. We make what we need. Nothing more, nothing less."
```

---

## SPACING & BREATHING ROOM

**White Space Strategy:**
- Generous margins (40-60px on desktop, responsive on mobile)
- 2-3 line breaks between major sections
- Extra space before/after pull quotes (24-32px)
- 1.5-2x space between body sections and content boxes
- No more than 65-75 characters per line for body text (optimal reading)

**Section Separation:**
- Use white space rather than visual dividers (lines, colors)
- Subheadings with natural breathing room above
- Numbered principles have clear visual spacing
- Box content (recipes, summaries) surrounded by white space for prominence

---

## RESTRAINED CTA STRUCTURE

**Philosophy:**
- No banners or pop-ups
- No aggressive language ("Shop Now!" becomes "Explore" or "Shop the Collection")
- CTAs appear naturally within editorial flow or at end
- Single CTA per article (maximum 2)
- Product linkage feels editorial, not commercial

**Example Format:**
```
WHILE YOU'RE HERE

[Product/Collection Name]
[Brief, benefit-driven description without hard sell]

[Link Text] →
```

**Tone:**
- Position as natural extension of reading experience
- "While you're here" creates warm, conversational feel
- Emphasize value and alignment with article topic
- Optional rather than demanded

## DESIGN TECHNICAL SPECS (for implementation)

**Colors:**
- Serif headlines: text-foreground (dark, premium)
- Body text: text-foreground, line-height 1.7
- Pull quotes: slightly larger serif, can be muted-foreground for distinction
- Captions/metadata: text-muted-foreground
- White space: background color
- Decorative accents: subtle, minimal

**Typography Implementation:**
- Serif font: Used for headlines, subheadings, pull quotes (e.g., Georgia, Lora, Garamond)
- Sans-serif: Body text and UI elements
- Font sizes scale responsively
- Line-height increases with longer-form content

**Layout Grid:**
- Center column (600-750px on desktop)
- Images can extend to 900px for impact
- Mobile: full width with side margins
- Flex-based for natural text flow

## ARTICLE TRANSFORMATION CHECKLIST

When redesigning or creating new articles, ensure they include:

- [ ] Personal narrative or anecdote opening (story-first)
- [ ] Clear hook/insight that contradicts conventional thinking
- [ ] Numbered principles or framework (if applicable)
- [ ] Deep-dive section with practical examples
- [ ] Recipe/box content with clear formatting
- [ ] Pull quote(s) that distill key learnings
- [ ] Integration/application to reader's life
- [ ] Minimal, restrained CTA (1-2 max)
- [ ] Generous white space and breathing room
- [ ] Strategic image placement (not clustered)
- [ ] Serif/sans-serif contrast for hierarchy
- [ ] 8-15 minute read time (includes pacing via white space)

## VOICE GUARDRAILS

**Do:**
- Tell stories before explaining concepts
- Show expertise through experience, not credentials
- Honor generational knowledge and cultural origins
- Make principles immediately practical
- Maintain conversational warmth
- Use specific sensory detail
- Let the value proposition emerge naturally

**Don't:**
- Open with definitions or theory
- Use corporate wellness language ("optimize," "biohack," etc.)
- Treat traditional knowledge as trend
- Overwhelm with information
- Create artificial urgency or scarcity
- Use "you must" — instead use "you might consider"
- Push products; position them as natural extensions of learning
